# Radar Interference Reduction Based on Golay Complementary Code for Pulse Radar Doppler Sidelobes Suppression [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract: To mitigate the interference from range-Doppler sidelobes in pulse radar systems, we propose a new method to construct Golay codes for Doppler elastic complementary waveforms. This algorithm optimizes both the transmitted pulse sequence and the received pulse weights, maximizing the similarity between the pulse weights and the given window function while satisfying multiple constraints on high-order Doppler nulls and energy. The problem is represented as an 0-1 integer programming problem, which is then solved using semidefinite relaxation and randomization techniques. This waveform achieves complete suppression of range sidelobes in multiple flexible and adjustable Doppler regions, demonstrating good performance in Doppler sidelobe suppression, Doppler resolution, and signal-to-noise ratio.
Keywords: Range-Doppler Sidelobe Suppression, Waveform Diversity and Design, Complementary Codes, Integer Programming
Existing methods cannot provide sufficient Doppler elasticity as required by many practical applications and lack comprehensive consideration of waveform performance. In this letter, we develop a new design algorithm for DRCW (Doppler Radar Combinatorial Waveform). We seek to maximize the similarity between the pulse weight and the energy of a given window function, subject to multiple constraints on high-order Doppler nulls. Both BD (Binary Design) and Max SNR (Maximum Signal-to-Noise Ratio) designs can be considered as special cases. The design task ultimately leads to an 0-1 integer programming problem. Due to the difficulty in obtaining global optimal solutions, suboptimal solutions are calculated through semidefinite programming and Gaussian randomization. The algorithm can design multiple DRCWs with flexible adjustable distance sidelobe masking areas, Doppler sidelobe suppression, controllable loss of signal-to-noise ratio and Doppler resolution.

Simulation experiment analysis
The waveform parameters are set as follows: Sequence length N = 64, pulse number M = 50, and the duty cycle of the transmitter (pulse duration divided by PRI) is 5%. The background contains many stationary strong reflectors such as buildings, where there are multiple slow-moving targets with Doppler shifts less than 0.2π rad and fast-moving targets with Doppler shifts around 0.8π rad. The speed of these fast moving targets is measured or provided by trackers. To achieve simultaneous detection of both types of targets, we set θ0 = 0 and θ1 = 0.8π. The width of each RSBA depends on the distribution and uncertainty of the velocity, for example, let K0 = 20, K1 = 4.
Two DRCWs were constructed using Hamming window and rectangular window templates respectively. The design results are shown in Figure 1. In Figure 2, cross-ambiguity functions for several (uS, uW) pairs are shown. Figure 3 gives their Doppler contours at k = 0 and the relationship between PRSL and Doppler shift. The results indicate that: (1) BD cannot provide the required Doppler elasticity, leading to significant decrease in SNR and Doppler resolution; (2) NM-DRCW can meet our requirements by setting two RSBAs, with a negligible side effect; (iii) NM-DRCW with Hamming window achieves lower Doppler sidelobes and wider RSBAs than NM-DRCW with rectangular window, while the latter performs better in terms of SNR and Doppler resolution. Performance metrics of the DRCWs constructed under various conditions are listed in Table I when K1 = 0 and K0 varies from 10 to 40. It is worth noting that NM-DRCW can achieve flexible compromises among various performance indicators.
